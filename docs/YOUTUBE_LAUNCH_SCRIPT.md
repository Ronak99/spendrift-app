# Spendrift — Launch video script (first person)

Natural voiceover for a product walkthrough. Demo the app on screen while reading; adjust names, amounts, and examples to match your recording.

---

## Cold open

Introducing **Spendrift** an app that I made, using which I track money across **several accounts**. I am not someone who can micromanage my expenses, I wanted an app which allows me to track an expense as it happens, I should be able to speak to the app or create an expense on the go. Let me demonstrate what I am talking about.

---

## Accounts

I always start with **accounts**, because everything is connected with them.

I’m going to add **HDFC Salary** and give it a starting balance that matches what I really have — say **₹45,000**.

Then I’ll add something like **Axis credit card** so spending on the card doesn’t get mixed into my bank balance.

I’ll pick which account feels like my **main** one — that’s the one quick shortcuts and imports default to, which keeps day-to-day logging fast.

---

## Categories

Under **Categories**, I set up buckets that match how *I* think about money — **Dining**, **Groceries**, **Coffee**, whatever. The ones I use all the time bubble up when I’m logging, so I’m not hunting through a long list every time.

---

## Transactions

The **Transactions** tab is my home base. I’m looking at **this month** — what went out, what came in, and what’s left in spirit at the top.

I can flip to **last month** with the arrows, jump back to **now** with a quick **double-tap** on the month, or **press and hold** if I want to jump to a specific month. I like that I can’t accidentally scroll into the future — it stays grounded in real months.

---

## Adding something by hand

I tap add and choose **Type**.

*“Let me log dinner — note: **Burma Burma with Aditi**, **₹1,850**, from **HDFC Salary**, category **Dining**.”*

Then maybe income: *“**Freelance — Acme**, **₹22,000**, income, same salary account.”*

I tap a row to **fix a mistake**, or **swipe** to delete if I duplicated something.

---

## Voice

Same plus button — **Speak**. I hold the mic and say something like: *“Three hundred rupees, Uber, transport.”* When I’m done, the app turns that into a transaction. If it mishears a name or amount, I get the option tweak it before saving — same as typing, just faster on the go.

---

## Bank statement

If I’ve got a **PDF** from the bank, I use **Upload document**. There’s a short **privacy note** — I read it, confirm I’m good with it, then pick the file. I choose **which account** those lines belong to, skim the **preview**, remove anything that’s not really a transaction, then save. New lines show up in my history; I can categorize them later if I want them in my charts.

---

## Shortcuts & widgets

I use **Siri Shortcuts** for the boring stuff — log an amount without opening the app, or talk an expense in from Shortcuts when my hands are full. On the **Home Screen**, the **widget** shows my main account snapshot and gives me a fast path to **log** or **capture** without digging through icons.

---

## Analytics

**Analytics** is where it gets honest: how much I **spent** and **earned**, **net** for the month, and a **daily average** so I know if I’m on a gentle drip or a fire hose. The **category breakdown** shows where the money actually went; **trends** show busy days and whether I’m a weekday spender or a weekend spender. If the month is empty, the charts stay quiet — fair enough.

---

## Settings

**Settings** is themes, Shortcuts links, sharing the app, and a place to **send feedback** if something bugs me or I want a feature. Keeps the “product” side out of the way until I need it.

---

## Sign-off

That’s **Spendrift** in practice: **accounts** first, **transactions** in the flow that fits you — **type**, **voice**, or **PDF** — and **analytics** so the month isn’t a mystery. If this sounds useful, it’s on the App Store — I’m [name], thanks for watching.
